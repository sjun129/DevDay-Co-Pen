# Legal & Institutional Requirements for Disclosing / Labeling / Recording AI-Generated Text (Co-Pen, status as of 2026-09-30)

> Not legal advice. Several items below come from secondary sources (law-firm alerts, news) and are flagged where the primary text was not directly verified in this session.

## 1. EU AI Act Article 50: obligations for AI-generated text, dates, Code of Practice, Digital Omnibus

### Takeaway
Article 50 has applied since 2 August 2026. Providers of generative systems must machine-mark text outputs (Art. 50(2)). Deployers must disclose AI-generated text published "to inform the public on matters of public interest" (Art. 50(4)), unless there was human review or editorial control and someone holds editorial responsibility. The Digital Omnibus (Reg. (EU) 2026/1744, in force 27 Jul 2026) left Article 50 alone apart from a grace period to 2 Dec 2026 for 50(2) marking on systems already on the market. For Co-Pen (a Korean student tool, not placed on the EU market, whose outputs are coursework rather than public-interest publications), Article 50 is most likely **not legally binding**. It is still the best reference design for provenance.

### Cited Findings
- **Art. 50(2), verbatim:** "Providers of AI systems, including general-purpose AI systems, generating synthetic audio, image, video or text content, shall ensure that the outputs of the AI system are marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as artificially generated or manipulated. Providers shall ensure their technical solutions are effective, interoperable, robust and reliable as far as this is technically feasible, taking into account the specificities and limitations of various types of content, the costs of implementation and the generally acknowledged state of the art…" — [artificialintelligenceact.eu, Art. 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)
- **Art. 50(2) exception:** "This obligation shall not apply to the extent the AI systems perform an assistive function for standard editing or do not substantially alter the input data provided by the deployer or the semantics thereof…" — [Art. 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)
- **Art. 50(4) second subparagraph, verbatim:** "Deployers of an AI system that generates or manipulates text which is published with the purpose of informing the public on matters of public interest shall disclose that the text has been artificially generated or manipulated. This obligation shall not apply … where the AI-generated content has undergone a process of human review or editorial control and where a natural or legal person holds editorial responsibility for the publication of the content." — [Art. 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)
- **Art. 50(1):** providers of AI systems that interact directly with natural persons must design them so people are told they are interacting with an AI system, unless that is obvious from the context. — [Art. 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)
- **Art. 50(5):** the information must be given "in a clear and distinguishable manner at the latest at the time of the first interaction or exposure." — [Art. 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)
- **Art. 50(7):** the Commission and AI Office encourage codes of practice on detecting and labelling AI-generated content. The Commission may adopt implementing acts if a code is inadequate. — [Art. 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)
- **Application date:** the Art. 50 transparency obligations apply "from 2 August 2026." — [European Commission, Digital Strategy news](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-publishes-code-practice-marking-and-labelling-ai-generated-content)
- **Final Code of Practice on marking and labelling AI-generated content:** published **10 June 2026**. It is voluntary, covers providers and deployers of generative AI systems, is open for signature, and is being assessed for adequacy by the Commission and the AI Board. — [EC news](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-publishes-code-practice-marking-and-labelling-ai-generated-content); [Jones Day, June 2026](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2026/06/european-commission-publishes-final-code-of-practice-on-marking-and-labelling-aigenerated-content)
- **Code, provider side:** "no single marking technique can fully meet the AI Act's requirements," so the Code uses a "multi-layered approach, requiring signatories to use at least two layers of machine-readable marking where necessary, such as combinations of metadata, watermarks, or other technical measures." It applies to text as well as audio, image and video. Providers must also offer detection mechanisms to deployers, users, authorities, researchers, civil society and media. — [Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2026/06/european-commission-publishes-final-code-of-practice-on-marking-and-labelling-aigenerated-content)
- **Code, deployer side:** label deepfakes and AI-generated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest, "unless the text has undergone human review or editorial control and editorial responsibility has been assumed." Guidance covers placement, the public **EU icon** or equivalent labels, and audio disclaimers. — [Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2026/06/european-commission-publishes-final-code-of-practice-on-marking-and-labelling-aigenerated-content)
- The Commission will also issue **guidelines** on the scope of Art. 50 and on matters the Code does not cover. — [Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2026/06/european-commission-publishes-final-code-of-practice-on-marking-and-labelling-aigenerated-content)
- **Earlier drafts:** the first draft Code on transparency of AI-generated content — [EC library](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/first-draft-code-practice-transparency-ai-generated-content); draft covered in [Jones Day, Jan 2026](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2026/01/european-commission-publishes-draft-code-of-practice-on-ai-labelling-and-transparency)
- **Digital Omnibus on AI = Regulation (EU) 2026/1744:** published in the OJ on 24 July 2026 and in force 27 July 2026. — [lawandtechnology.eu](https://lawandtechnology.eu/en/digital-omnibus-on-ai-official-journal-regulation-2026-1744/); [Cuatrecasas](https://www.cuatrecasas.com/en/global/intellectual-property/art/digital-omnibus-ai-has-been-published); [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/eu-digital-omnibus-on-ai-enters-into-force)
- **Omnibus and Art. 50:** "AI systems that have been placed on the market before 2 August 2026 will benefit from a four-month grace period (until 2 December 2026)" for Art. 50(2) watermarking. "The broader Article 50 transparency obligations … remain unaffected and proceed as scheduled from 2 August 2026." Systems placed on the market on or after 2 Aug 2026 get no grace period. — [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/); [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/); [CSA Research Note](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-eu-ai-act-article50-watermarking-deadline/)
- **Omnibus and high-risk systems (context):** Annex III systems move to 2 Dec 2027 and Annex I systems to 2 Aug 2028. — [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/)
- **Source conflict:** Usercentrics still says the Code of Practice "remains in development" with "the final version … expected later this year." That contradicts the EC's 10 June 2026 publication and looks like stale text. — [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/) vs [EC](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-publishes-code-practice-marking-and-labelling-ai-generated-content)

### Inferences
- **Role mapping.** Co-Pen agents call an upstream LLM (e.g., Claude/GPT). The model vendor is the GPAI provider. Co-Pen itself is arguably the *provider of an AI system* (the editor with agents), and a student team that publishes output would be the *deployer*. Coursework submitted to a professor is not "published with the purpose of informing the public on matters of public interest," so Art. 50(4) text disclosure most likely does not apply to assignments.
- **The Art. 50(4) editorial-control exception matches Co-Pen's accept-flow closely.** A logged record that a named human accepted, reviewed or edited each AI suggestion is the kind of evidence of "human review or editorial control" that the exception depends on. The provenance mark should therefore store **accepted_by, accepted_at, and whether the text was edited after acceptance**, not just "AI wrote this."
- The Art. 50(2) "assistive function for standard editing" exception may cover light features such as grammar fixes and rephrasing. Full paragraph generation would not fall under it. Recording an **operation type** per suggestion (generate / rewrite / proofread) lets Co-Pen tell these apart.
- The Code's "at least two layers" guidance suggests a design of (1) structured metadata in the document model or export (e.g., C2PA/JSON manifest) plus (2) a second layer such as an invisible in-text mark or a server-side log/fingerprint lookup. Co-Pen's plan of invisible marks plus an append-only audit log already follows this two-layer pattern.
- Applicability to Co-Pen is low. Under AI Act Art. 2, obligations attach to placing a system on the EU market or to outputs used in the EU. A Korean-only hackathon prototype is very likely out of scope. (Art. 2 was not re-fetched in this session; see [Art. 2](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/2/).)

### Gaps
- The full text of the final Code (specific text-marking commitments such as fingerprinting/logging and minimum robustness) was not read directly. Only the EC news page and law-firm summaries were used.
- The Art. 50 Commission guidelines had no publication date or status found as of Sept 2026.
- The Omnibus Art. 50(2) grace-period wording was confirmed only through secondary sources (Gibson Dunn, Usercentrics, CSA), not from the EUR-Lex text of Reg. 2026/1744.
- No list of Code signatories or AI Board adequacy decision was found.

## 2. Korea AI Basic Act (인공지능 발전과 신뢰 기반 조성 등에 관한 기본법): transparency duties, 시행령, guidelines, 계도기간, applicability

### Takeaway
The Act has been in force since **22 Jan 2026**. Art. 31 requires 인공지능사업자 to (①) notify users in advance that a product or service runs on generative/high-impact AI, (②) indicate that outputs were generated by generative AI, and (③) clearly notify or label realistic synthetic content (deepfakes). Violating ① can bring a fine of up to KRW 30 million. The government is running a 계도기간 of **at least one year**, so fines are unlikely before 2027. The Enforcement Decree exempts cases where AI use is obvious and internal-only use. Users who merely use AI as a work tool are not obligated. A student hackathon demo is probably low-risk, but Co-Pen as a *service offered to other students* would technically fall into the "AI business operator" role.

### Cited Findings
- **Art. 31① (verbatim):** "인공지능사업자는 고영향 인공지능이나 생성형 인공지능을 이용한 제품 또는 서비스를 제공하려는 경우 제품 또는 서비스가 해당 인공지능에 기반하여 운용된다는 사실을 이용자에게 사전에 고지하여야 한다." — [국가법령정보센터 제31조](https://www.law.go.kr/LSW//lsSideInfoP.do?lsiSeq=282791&joNo=0031&joBrNo=00&docCls=jo&urlMode=lsScJoRltInfoR)
- **Art. 31②:** "인공지능사업자는 생성형 인공지능 또는 이를 이용한 제품 또는 서비스를 제공하는 경우 그 결과물이 생성형 인공지능에 의하여 생성되었다는 사실을 표시하여야 한다." — [law.go.kr 제31조](https://www.law.go.kr/LSW//lsSideInfoP.do?lsiSeq=282791&joNo=0031&joBrNo=00&docCls=jo&urlMode=lsScJoRltInfoR)
- **Art. 31③:** applies to realistic synthetic sound, image or video, which must be notified or labeled "이용자가 명확하게 인식할 수 있는 방식으로". **Art. 31④:** methods and exceptions are delegated to Presidential Decree. — [law.go.kr 제31조](https://www.law.go.kr/LSW//lsSideInfoP.do?lsiSeq=282791&joNo=0031&joBrNo=00&docCls=jo&urlMode=lsScJoRltInfoR)
- **Enforcement date 22 Jan 2026.** Generative AI services must notify users in advance. Deepfakes need clearly recognizable marks that consider user age. Other outputs (e.g., animation, webtoons) may use visible marks or "눈에 보이지 않는 디지털 워터마크". UI elements and pop-up notices are also accepted. — [대한민국 정책브리핑 korea.kr](https://www.korea.kr/news/policyNewsView.do?newsId=148958380)
- **계도기간:** "최소 1년 이상." 사실조사 (fact-finding investigations) only in "극히 예외적인 경우" such as loss of life or human-rights harm. — [korea.kr](https://www.korea.kr/news/policyNewsView.do?newsId=148958380); [Kim & Chang, 2026-01-23](https://www.kimchang.com/ko/insights/detail.kc?sch_section=4&idx=34019)
- **Fine:** failing to give the Art. 31① notice brings a 과태료 of up to KRW 30 million. Commentators expect actual fines no earlier than 2027. — [Lexology/law-firm commentary via search](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=d3fa41a1-f09a-44da-8361-4e1eb7c61041); [law.go.kr 제43조 (과태료)](https://www.law.go.kr/LSW//lsSideInfoP.do?lsiSeq=282791&joNo=0043&joBrNo=00&docCls=jo&urlMode=lsScJoRltInfoR) *(amount taken from search snippets, not read verbatim)*
- **Revised 인공지능 투명성 확보 가이드라인 (Kim & Chang summary):**
  - Inside the service environment, **"유연한 표시"** (flexible labeling) is allowed: UI symbols, logos, or pre-use notices.
  - For outputs **exported outside** the service (download or share), **"엄격한 표시"** (strict labeling) is required, by one of two methods:
    - (a) human-perceptible: a visible or audible watermark plus text or voice guidance;
    - (b) machine-readable: metadata **plus** a notice to the user.
  - Text outputs are covered when exported. — [Kim & Chang](https://www.kimchang.com/ko/insights/detail.kc?sch_section=4&idx=34019)
- **Who is obligated:** operators that "직접 제공하는" AI products or services to users. The guidelines say that users who only use AI as a work "도구" are **excluded**. The obligation generally falls on the final business that provides the service to the 이용자. — [Kim & Chang](https://www.kimchang.com/ko/insights/detail.kc?sch_section=4&idx=34019); [Lexology 해설 시리즈(1)](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=132014d5-784b-41ab-9826-b6cbb2e76ce7) *(403 on fetch; snippet only)*
- **Enforcement Decree 제22조④ exceptions (per search summaries):**
  - (1) AI use is obvious from the product or service name, the user screen, or packaging text;
  - (2) the AI is used **only for the operator's internal business**. — [Kim & Chang 시행령 초안](https://www.kimchang.com/ko/insights/detail.kc?sch_section=4&idx=32907); [korea.kr 시행령 입법예고 news](https://www.korea.kr/news/policyNewsView.do?newsId=148954629); [ZDNet 2025-12-24](https://zdnet.co.kr/view/?no=20251224132637) *(article number and wording not verified against the final promulgated decree)*
- **July 2026:** further 후속 법령 (subordinate rules) took effect on 21 July 2026, including an AI product and service confirmation system and public-procurement preference. Industry still reports confusion over "생성형 AI를 어느 정도 활용해야 표시 의무가 발생하는지" (how much generative AI use triggers the labeling duty) and whether the same standard applies "사람이 결과물을 편집하거나 검수한 경우" (when a person has edited or reviewed the output). The 계도기간 is restated as at least one year. — [경향신문 2026-07-21](https://www.khan.co.kr/article/202607210908001/); [Daum/뉴스 2026-07-21](https://v.daum.net/v/20260721053152326)
- A support desk (인공지능기본법 지원데스크, run through KOSA) offers compliance consulting. — [korea.kr](https://www.korea.kr/news/policyNewsView.do?newsId=148958380); [Kim & Chang](https://www.kimchang.com/ko/insights/detail.kc?sch_section=4&idx=34019)

### Inferences
- **Does it apply to Co-Pen?** The Act has no explicit exemption for non-commercial or student projects found in these sources. If Co-Pen is offered to other students as a service (even free), the team arguably acts as an AI business operator that provides generative AI, triggering Art. 31①/②. An internal team demo at DevDay likely falls under the "internal use" or "AI use is obvious" exceptions, and the 계도기간 makes enforcement practically nil in 2026. Treat compliance as a **low-cost best practice**, not a legal risk.
- **Cheap features that cover Art. 31 in full:**
  - (a) a first-use notice / onboarding banner saying agents are generative AI (Art. 31①);
  - (b) in-editor visual badges on AI paragraphs (in-service "flexible" labeling, Art. 31②);
  - (c) on export (DOCX/PDF/HWP), embed machine-readable metadata **and** show the user a notice, or add a visible "AI 생성 포함" footer. This matches the guideline's "strict labeling" for exported outputs. The "AI 사용 내역서" export can serve as that user-facing notice.
- The open question over human-edited outputs (see the July 2026 articles) means Co-Pen should record the **human edit ratio** per AI-originated paragraph, so a report can say "AI 초안 → 사람 수정 N%."
- The students who submit assignments are themselves "users using AI as a tool," so they have no AI Basic Act duty. Their duties come from university policy (Section 3).

### Gaps
- The final promulgated 시행령 article text on labeling methods and exceptions was not verified on law.go.kr.
- No authoritative statement was found on whether free, non-profit or student services count as 인공지능사업자 (the Art. 2 definition was not fetched).
- No exact end date for the 계도기간 was found. It is described only as "최소 1년 이상" from 22 Jan 2026.
- No guideline text was found on how much human editing removes the labeling duty. Sources say this is still unclear as of July 2026.

## 3. Korean university / Ministry of Education guidance on disclosing AI use in coursework (incl. Dong-A University)

### Takeaway
The Ministry of Education's non-binding 「대학 AI 활용 윤리 가이드라인」 (draft 27 Feb 2026, final distribution planned for Apr–May 2026) lets instructors set a no / limited / free-use policy. Instructors may *require* citation or source notes for AI use and *optionally* require an **"AI 활용 보고서"** (AI use report) describing the process, method and contribution. Using AI without proper acknowledgment is misuse. University guidelines (Yonsei 2024-05, SNU 2026-03-18, Korea Univ. 2023-03, Dongseo) generally ask for the tool/model, prompt, date and platform. **No official Dong-A University generative AI guideline was found.**

### Cited Findings
- **교육부 「대학 AI 활용 윤리 가이드라인」:** draft announced **2026-02-27**, final distribution expected "4~5월." Instructors may "참고 문헌 기록(출처 및 인용 표기 등)을 필수적으로 요구할 수 있다" (require reference records such as source and citation marks). They may also optionally require an "AI 활용 과정과 방법, 기여도를 기술한 'AI 활용 보고서'" (an AI use report describing the process, method and contribution). Misuse is defined as when the student "하나 이상의 AI 도구를 사용했으나 사용 사실을 적절하게 인정하지 않고" submits work (used one or more AI tools without properly acknowledging it). Instructors may include "AI 활용 여부, 활용 방식, 생성물 출처 명시" (whether AI was used, how, and source attribution of outputs) in grading criteria. The 12 detailed principles include 인용 표기 (citation) and 사용 사실·과정 공개 (disclosing the fact and process of use). — [교수신문](https://www.kyosu.net/news/articleView.html?idxno=200008)
- The guideline is **not legally binding**. Universities use it as a reference for their own rules. It sets three usage tiers ("사용 금지 / 제한적 사용 / 사용 허용") and says submitting AI output without review or revision may count as cheating. — [search summary of SNU 화학교육과 notice](https://chemedu.snu.ac.kr/13462/); [머니투데이 2026-02-26](https://www.mt.co.kr/policy/2026/02/26/2026022610083850284) *(details from search snippets; confirm the final version)*
- **Yonsei University:** 「생성형 AI 활용 가이드라인」 ver 1.0, May 2024 (notice dated 2024-05-29). The course syllabus should state whether AI is allowed. Citation includes the generative AI model, prompt, and platform. — [Yonsei notice](https://digitalint.yonsei.ac.kr/board/notice.php?mNum=4&sNum=1&boardid=notice&mode=view&idx=6); [Yonsei PDF](https://socsci.yonsei.ac.kr/socsci/community/notice.do?mode=download&articleNo=448211&attachNo=191152) *(citation fields from search summary; PDF not read)*
- **Seoul National University:** 「서울대학교 AI 가이드라인」 enacted **2026-03-18** (Korean and English PDFs). It covers transparency, accuracy, privacy and security, and accountability across education, research and administration. — [SNU downloads](https://www.snu.ac.kr/about/downloads?md=v&bbsidx=166508) *(PDF contents not read)*
- **Korea University:** first "ChatGPT guideline" in March 2023. Instructors state AI-use principles in the syllabus and communicate them to students. — [서울신문 2023-03-17](https://www.seoul.co.kr/news/society/2023/03/17/20230317010008)
- **Dongseo University (동서대, Busan; *not* Dong-A):** students must disclose the tool name (e.g., "ChatGPT 4"), the prompt, the date of use (e.g., "2024. 06. 01"), and the URL. Submitting unmodified AI output is plagiarism and academic misconduct, and the student is personally responsible. — [동서대 가이드라인](https://www.dongseo.ac.kr/kr/index.php?pCode=guide_line01)
- **Dong-A University (동아대학교):** targeted searches found no official generative AI coursework guideline on donga.ac.kr. One search-engine summary attributed plagiarism rules to "Dong-A," but that summary cited Dongseo's page, so it is a **misattribution**. — [search results incl. donga.ac.kr pages](https://software.donga.ac.kr/); [Dongseo page](https://www.dongseo.ac.kr/kr/index.php?pCode=guide_line01)
- Other universities with guidelines include 중앙대 [CAU AI guide](https://aiguide.cau.ac.kr/bm/bm-1.php), 가톨릭대 [CUK CTL](https://ctl.cuk.edu/cms/FrCon/index.do?MENU_ID=330), 충남대 [CNU](https://ile.cnu.ac.kr/ile/ai/guideline-rule.do), and 한국체대 [KNSU](https://www.knsu.ac.kr/ctl/edu/ai-edu.do).

### Inferences
- **Recommended "AI 사용 내역서" fields**, mapped to MoE, Yonsei and Dongseo requirements:
  - tool / model name and version, plus platform (e.g., "Co-Pen agent 'Researcher' → Claude model X");
  - date and time of each generation;
  - the prompt / instruction given to the agent;
  - which paragraphs were AI-originated, with location;
  - who accepted each suggestion and when;
  - how much the human changed it afterward (contribution / 기여도);
  - an overall AI contribution ratio;
  - the course's usage tier (금지 / 제한 / 허용) as declared by the student.
- The MoE "AI 활용 보고서 (과정·방법·기여도)" concept maps directly onto Co-Pen's audit log. Co-Pen can generate this report automatically, which is a strong DevDay pitch point.
- Since per-course policy decides what is allowed, Co-Pen should let a team set the **course AI policy tier** at document creation. In "금지" (prohibited) mode it could disable agents or warn.
- Citation formats differ by institution. The export should support a configurable citation template, e.g., Dongseo/APA-like "Tool(date). 'prompt'. Vendor. URL."
- Team assignments raise attribution questions *between students* too. Logging which human accepted or edited each AI paragraph supports fair peer contribution reporting.

### Gaps
- Dong-A University policy is unknown. The team should ask Dong-A's 교육혁신원 / 교수학습개발센터 (teaching and learning center) directly or check LMS notices. It may exist only inside the LMS (not publicly indexed).
- The final MoE guideline (post Apr–May 2026) was not located. Only the Feb 2026 draft reporting was found.
- The actual SNU and Yonsei PDF contents (exact citation fields) were not read.
- No Korean university was found that requires prompts to be *attached* rather than cited. This could not be confirmed.

## 4. Content provenance standards for text: C2PA / Content Credentials, SynthID Text — feasibility

### Takeaway
C2PA added a method for embedding manifests in **unstructured (plain) text** (v2.3, refined in 2.4). It encodes the signed manifest as invisible Unicode variation-selector characters, and open-source libraries exist, so a small team can implement it. SynthID Text is open source (HF Transformers ≥4.46) but works as a **generation-time logits processor**. Co-Pen can only use it if it runs its own open-weight model; it cannot add SynthID to Claude or GPT API output. It is also weak on short or factual text and after rewriting or translation.

### Cited Findings
- The C2PA spec (current 2.4) includes embedding manifests in unstructured text. The manifest store (JUMBF) is wrapped (C2PATextManifestWrapper) and encoded as **non-rendering Unicode variation selectors**, with a data-hash hard binding. The spec says this method should be used only where no other embedding is feasible, and structured-text methods are preferred for code, config and markup. — [C2PA Spec 2.4](https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.4/specs/C2PA_Specification.html); [encypherai/c2pa-text reference impl](https://github.com/encypherai/c2pa-text); [writerslogic/c2pa-unstructured-text](https://github.com/writerslogic/c2pa-unstructured-text)
- Open implementations exist in Rust ([lib.rs c2pa-text](https://lib.rs/crates/c2pa-text)), Go ([pkg.go.dev](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/encypherai/c2pa-text/go/v2/c2pa_text)), and for HTML and structured text ([c2pa-html](https://github.com/writerslogic/c2pa-html), [c2pa-structured-text](https://github.com/writerslogic/c2pa-structured-text)).
- A Unicode Technical Committee document (L2/26-042) discusses embedded metadata in "plain" text. This indicates the practice is still under standards discussion. — [unicode.org L2/26-042](https://www.unicode.org/L2/L2026/26042-embedded-metadata-in-plain-text.pdf)
- **SynthID Text:** a logits processor that "augments the model's logits using a pseudorandom g-function." It needs access to the generation pipeline, HF Transformers v4.46.0+, and secure key storage (`keys`, `ngram_len`=5 recommended). A Bayesian detector returns watermarked / not watermarked / uncertain. Limitations: it is weaker on "factual responses," and confidence drops after "thorough rewriting, or translation to another language." — [Google AI for Developers, SynthID](https://ai.google.dev/responsible/docs/safeguards/synthid)
- SynthID Text is open source (Transformers ≥4.46, GitHub). Through the Gemini API and chat, detection is binary with no scores exposed. — [c9group explainer](https://c9group.dev/en/articles/synthid-text-watermarking-detection-guide) *(secondary)*
- OpenAI reportedly shelved a text watermark over robustness to translation and rewriting and over concerns about flagging non-native English writers. — [c9group / search summary](https://c9group.dev/en/articles/synthid-text-watermarking-detection-guide) *(secondary; unverified)*

### Inferences
- **Feasible for Co-Pen:**
  - (1) Structured provenance in the CRDT/document model: node attributes such as `{agent_id, model, prompt_id, accepted_by, accepted_at, edited_ratio}`. This is the simplest and most robust inside the editor.
  - (2) On export, a C2PA-style manifest (sidecar JSON or an embedded manifest for DOCX/PDF). Variation-selector text embedding is optional for copy-paste survival.
  - (3) A hash-chained append-only audit log as the server-side layer.
  
  Together these give the "two-layer" pattern suggested by the EU Code and the metadata-plus-notice method in the Korean guideline.
- **Caveat on invisible Unicode marks:** they are easy to strip (retyping, some sanitizers, HWP conversion), may break search, word counts, or plagiarism checkers, and could look like evasion or "hidden text" to instructors. Always pair them with a visible disclosure (the 내역서) and document them openly.
- **SynthID Text is not practical** unless Co-Pen self-hosts an open model. Korean-language robustness is also unproven in these sources. Skip it for the hackathon and mention it as future work.
- Watermark detection is not reliable for proving or disproving AI use in coursework, which argues for *process logs* (who, what, when) over *content forensics*. This is Co-Pen's differentiator.

### Gaps
- The C2PA version and release date that introduced unstructured-text embedding (2.3 per secondary sources) were not confirmed from the C2PA changelog.
- No evidence was found on SynthID Text performance on Korean.
- No evaluation was found of whether variation-selector marks survive HWP, Google Docs or LMS upload pipelines used by Korean universities.
